NAME: werewolf humanoid
ALIASES: wolfman; lycanthrope; humanoid wolf; 늑대인간; 웨어울프; 인간형 늑대
CATEGORY: creature / humanoid monster

FUNCTION:
- serves as a readable predator/prey or horror antagonist with both human posture and wolf identity
- can show fear, pursuit, injury, or aggression through human-like gesture plus animal head, fur, claws, and digitigrade legs

CORE_SILHOUETTE:
- broad-shouldered humanoid body with wolf head, pointed ears, snout, clawed hands, fur mane, and bent animal-like legs
- reads as a human-wolf hybrid, not a normal wolf on two legs or a masked human

PRIMARY_FORMS:
- wolf skull / snout wedge
- pointed ears
- thick neck and fur ruff
- humanoid ribcage and pelvis
- long arms / clawed hands
- digitigrade or strongly bent legs
- tail or trailing fur mass when used
- costume/straps/armor if present

DIMENSIONAL_ENVELOPE:
- generally taller, bulkier, and longer-limbed than a normal human unless intentionally weak or fleeing
- head and hands/feet can be larger than human proportion, but body joints must still connect believably

THICKNESS_NOTES:
- neck, shoulders, forearms, thighs, and calves carry muscular/fur thickness
- snout has solid skull depth; ears are thin triangular planes
- claws are thin tapered extensions, not extra fingers

PROPORTION_RULES:
- snout length must remain tied to skull and jaw hinge
- shoulders and ribcage can be larger than pelvis for beast read
- legs may be digitigrade, but knees/ankles must not become random bends
- if an arm is missing, the shoulder/stump/sleeve silhouette must clarify absence without graphic gore unless explicitly allowed

HUMAN_SCALE_READ:
- should feel physically larger or more dangerous than a normal person unless the story calls for a cornered, diminished creature
- in chase scenes, scale next to the human pursuer and rooftop edge must remain consistent

SUPPORT_LOGIC:
- running posture uses clear shoulder-pelvis counter-swing, planted or airborne feet, and forward/fleeing weight line
- claws, feet, and tail should follow motion direction
- clothing straps or bondage/fetish-inspired gear must wrap around torso/limbs with tension and attachment logic, not float as random bands

MATERIALS:
- fur, skin, claws, teeth, leather straps, metal buckles, torn fabric, bandages when injured

VISUAL_CUES:
- wolf snout
- pointed ears
- fur ruff
- clawed hands/feet
- hunched shoulders
- tail or fur silhouette
- fearful backward glance when fleeing

ANGLE_NOTES:
- front: snout symmetry, chest width, and arm placement matter most
- side: snout length, digitigrade leg chain, and tail/motion line matter most
- 3/4: best for chase scenes because face fear, torso twist, and running legs can all read

DO_NOT_BREAK:
- do not lose the wolf head silhouette
- do not turn digitigrade legs into rubber noodles
- do not let straps float without wrapping the body
- do not show graphic gore unless the brief specifically permits it; a missing limb can be handled with a dark stump, torn sleeve, bandage, or silhouette absence
- do not make fear expression so cartoony that threat identity disappears

STYLE_VARIATION:
- realistic: more anatomy, fur clumping, jaw structure, and muscular limb transitions
- anime simplified: strong head silhouette, fur mane, claws, and clear running pose
- gothic: darker fur, sharper claws, leather straps, heavy shadow, frightened eyes
- redjuice-inspired: black fur planes, white/cyan eye glints, rough broken contours, restrained but intense expression

SCENE_USAGE:
- use for rooftop chase, alley horror, gothic fairy-tale inversion, monster hunter scenes, and predator/prey reversals
- pair with anatomy body cards for humanoid pose, clothing/strap cards when needed, and rooftop/city cards for scale

SOURCE_NOTES:
- Generalized mythic werewolf structure for illustration workflow; focuses on silhouette, hybrid anatomy, and chase readability

CONFIDENCE: medium
