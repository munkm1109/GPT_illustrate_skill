NAME: geta sandal
ALIASES: geta; wooden geta; Japanese wooden sandal; geta sandals; 게다; 게다 샌들; 일본 나막신
CATEGORY: fashion / footwear

FUNCTION:
- lifts the foot above wet or dirty ground while keeping a traditional Japanese footwear silhouette
- implies ceremony, seasonal dress, kimono styling, and audible step rhythm

CORE_SILHOUETTE:
- narrow wooden top board with a thong strap and two downward teeth underneath
- reads like a raised flip-flop with a flat deck and visible under-support blocks
- in side or 3/4 view the front tooth usually sits slightly behind the toe area rather than at the very tip

PRIMARY_FORMS:
- dai (top board / foot platform)
- ha (two wooden teeth underneath)
- hanao (V-shaped strap with toe post)
- slightly beveled front and rear edges

DIMENSIONAL_ENVELOPE:
- common women's draw-read: roughly foot-length board around 220-240 mm long, about 80-95 mm wide, with visible lift around 35-55 mm depending on style
- the board stays noticeably narrower than many western sandals, especially through the waist

THICKNESS_NOTES:
- top board should feel like a solid carved slab, not a paper-thin sole
- each tooth usually reads chunkier than a chopstick but slimmer than a full heel block
- hanao strap should feel padded enough to visibly rise off the board

PROPORTION_RULES:
- the board is longer than it is wide, with a calm rectangle-to-oval hybrid profile in women's versions
- the two teeth should align along the long axis and not sit flush with the very front/back edges
- the strap attachment point sits between the big toe and second toe, with rear anchors pulled back toward mid-foot

HUMAN_SCALE_READ:
- reads as a one-foot object slightly longer than the visible foot from some angles
- height should be obvious next to bare toes and ankle, but not so tall that it becomes stilt-like unless intentionally stylized

SUPPORT_LOGIC:
- the wearer's weight rests on the top board while the two teeth transfer load to the ground
- in airborne or dangling poses, show the underside teeth clearly so the object still reads as geta rather than a flat sandal
- if the foot tilts, the deck and both teeth should still align like a single carved unit

MATERIALS:
- wood, often paulownia or other light carved wood logic
- fabric-covered or braided hanao strap
- optional lacquer, stain, or decorative textile strap finish

VISUAL_CUES:
- two-tooth underside
- visible thong strap separation between first and second toe
- flat deck with slightly softened edges
- wood grain or lacquer sheen
- traditional narrow profile

ANGLE_NOTES:
- front: toe post and narrow deck width matter most
- side: show the gap between deck and ground created by the two teeth
- 3/4: best for reading deck shape, tooth placement, and the V-strap simultaneously

DO_NOT_BREAK:
- do not flatten the sole into a modern flip-flop or wedge
- do not hide the two-tooth logic if the sandal is meant to read as geta
- the strap must split between the first and second toe, not like a side sandal strap
- board thickness and tooth logic must feel carved and load-bearing

STYLE_VARIATION:
- realistic: wood grain, subtle wear, strap compression, cleaner tooth geometry
- anime simplified: keep the board + two teeth + hanao read with fewer surface details
- gothic: darker lacquer, black-red strap textile, sharper edge accents
- neon sci-fi: retain the traditional silhouette but allow controlled glow edging or reflective lacquer treatment

SCENE_USAGE:
- excellent for character identity when the costume has kimono or ritual styling
- underside tooth rhythm helps a dangling or airborne foot read cleanly
- wood can catch small edge highlights without overpowering the face
