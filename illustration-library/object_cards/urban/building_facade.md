[OBJECT_CARD]

NAME: building facade
ALIASES: exterior wall; city building face; street facade; urban facade; 건물 외벽; 빌딩 파사드; 건물 정면
CATEGORY: urban / architecture / exterior surface

FUNCTION:
- provides the main vertical plane that anchors streets, alleys, rail corridors, signage, windows, fire escapes, balconies, and cables
- gives the scene human-scale repetition through floors, windows, doors, panels, and mounted fixtures
- acts as a perspective and depth ruler so figures, vehicles, and signs do not float in generic background texture

CORE_SILHOUETTE:
- large vertical slab or stack of slabs with repeated rectangular openings and structural edges
- reads first as a plane with rhythm, not as random small marks

PRIMARY_FORMS:
- main wall plane
- floor bands or horizontal story divisions
- window grid / openings
- pilasters, columns, corner edges, or panel seams
- door / storefront / service entry zones when near street level
- mounted objects such as signs, lights, cables, conduits, balconies, fire escapes, or air-conditioning units

DIMENSIONAL_ENVELOPE:
- height can range from one story to high-rise tower scale; draw-read should still preserve story rhythm
- depth is usually shallow at the facade face but may include protruding balconies, signs, pipes, frames, and ledges
- individual floors generally read taller than a standing adult and much taller than a head; windows and doors set the local human scale

THICKNESS_NOTES:
- wall edges, window recesses, sills, frames, ledges, and sign brackets need visible thickness in 3/4 view
- a facade should not be a paper-flat texture unless it is deliberately a distant silhouette
- protruding frames and panels help sell depth even with simplified anime rendering

PROPORTION_RULES:
- repeated windows and floor bands should follow one perspective system
- vertical spacing between floors must remain rhythmic unless the building is visibly damaged or stylized
- doors, windows, and mounted signs should agree with human scale and story height
- facade modules should align to the same building plane, not each float at separate angles

HUMAN_SCALE_READ:
- doors are roughly body-height plus clearance, windows are torso-to-body sized depending on building type, and floor bands exceed a standing person
- a nearby adult should not look larger than a full floor module unless the camera is extremely close and clearly foreshortened
- mounted signs and window bands can act as scale rulers beside characters and vehicles

SUPPORT_LOGIC:
- facade stands as a vertical wall plane tied to ground, street, or elevated corridor edges
- signs, cables, lamps, and pipes attach to the facade through brackets, holes, clips, conduits, or ledges
- balconies, fire escapes, or heavy signs need visible support, not floating placement

MATERIALS:
- concrete, brick, stone, painted plaster, metal cladding, glass, weathered tile, exposed steel, or composite panels
- wet surfaces catch vertical streaks and edge highlights; older surfaces collect grime beneath ledges and around fixtures

VISUAL_CUES:
- repeated window rhythm
- story bands
- corner edges
- ledges, sills, vents, conduits, brackets, and mounted signs
- grime streaks, rain runoff, cracks, chipped paint, or glass reflections

ANGLE_NOTES:
- front: grid rhythm and story spacing matter most
- side: wall thickness, protrusions, and attached fixtures matter most
- 3/4: best for street and rail scenes because perspective, window depth, and mounted object support can all read
- low angle: verticals may converge, but floor rhythm should remain understandable
- high angle: roof ledges, window recesses, and sign brackets become important scale cues

DO_NOT_BREAK:
- do not turn the facade into unanchored random rectangles
- do not let each window follow a different perspective
- do not float signs, cables, balconies, or lights with no attachment logic
- do not make windows, doors, and floors contradict the character or vehicle scale
- do not fill the facade with fake text or meaningless glyph noise when structure is the real need

STYLE_VARIATION:
- realistic: stronger material weathering, deeper window recesses, more believable fixture support
- anime simplified: clear slab, few window rows, selective seams, and larger shape rhythm before detail
- gothic urban fantasy: taller dark slabs, sharper arches or verticals, heavier shadow under ledges
- neon sci-fi: glass/metal panels, LED strips, mounted sign frames, and cool reflective edge planes
- redjuice-inspired: dark architectural planes cut by white/cyan edge glints, labels and signage only as controlled support accents

SCENE_USAGE:
- use as the default urban vertical plane behind characters, vehicles, rail corridors, alleys, and rooftop scenes
- excellent for establishing scale relationships between figure, vehicle, street, and city
- keep the clearest facade rhythm outside the face silhouette; allow detail to frame, not swallow, the focal figure
- pairs with `window_grid`, `transit_signage_glyph_panel`, `cable_bundle`, `glass_reflection_panel`, and rail/road objects

SOURCE_NOTES:
- Generalized from common urban exterior drawing logic: facade plane first, repeated openings second, mounted fixtures third
- Intended for drawability and scale locking rather than one architectural style or code-compliant building specification

CONFIDENCE: high
