NAME: highrise rooftop edge
ALIASES: skyscraper roof edge; rooftop parapet; building-top ledge; roof ledge; 고층빌딩 옥상 난간; 옥상 가장자리; 마천루 옥상
CATEGORY: urban / architecture / rooftop support plane

FUNCTION:
- provides a dangerous elevated support plane for running, jumping, chasing, dueling, or overlooking a city canyon
- makes height legible through parapets, roof slabs, HVAC units, safety rails, drop shadows, and distant street scale far below
- anchors foot contact and scale so airborne figures do not float in empty skyline texture

CORE_SILHOUETTE:
- flat roof plane ending in a sharp parapet or ledge line, often with a vertical drop beyond it
- reads as a hard horizontal/diagonal platform against deep city void

PRIMARY_FORMS:
- roof slab / surface plane
- parapet wall or coping stone
- ledge lip / edge thickness
- rooftop fixtures such as vents, HVAC boxes, antenna bases, access doors, rails, or warning lights
- vertical building face dropping below the ledge

DIMENSIONAL_ENVELOPE:
- roof surface can be broad enough for several people to run, but the ledge remains narrow and dangerous
- parapets often sit around knee-to-waist height in draw-read logic; low ledges still need thickness
- the drop below should dwarf people and props when the scene emphasizes height

THICKNESS_NOTES:
- roof slab, parapet cap, ledge lip, and wall face need visible thickness in 3/4 or high-angle views
- rooftop fixtures have boxy depth and should sit on the roof plane with contact shadows

PROPORTION_RULES:
- roof plane, parapet line, and building facade edges must share the same perspective system
- people’s feet should occupy small patches of the roof, not span the entire roof width
- far street/crowd scale should be tiny compared with rooftop figures

HUMAN_SCALE_READ:
- an adult can stand or sprint on the roof surface; parapets and doors act as local scale rulers
- distant ground-level people should become small clusters or dots when the drop is “dizzyingly far”

SUPPORT_LOGIC:
- feet contact the roof plane; jumps start from or land toward clear roof/ledge surfaces
- parapets, rails, vents, and signs attach to the slab or wall, not free-floating skyline
- if a character is near the edge, their weight line and foot direction must show risk without impossible balance

MATERIALS:
- concrete, asphalt membrane, metal coping, wet gravel, safety paint, steel rail, rooftop machinery

VISUAL_CUES:
- parapet lip
- roof seams
- vent/HVAC boxes
- antenna bases
- edge shadow
- tiny city/crowd far below

ANGLE_NOTES:
- high angle: ledge thickness and vertical drop matter most
- low angle: parapet silhouette and skyline separation matter most
- 3/4: best for chase scenes because roof plane, drop, and facade depth read together

DO_NOT_BREAK:
- do not make the roof a floating plank with no building mass below
- do not let feet hover without contact shadows or roof-plane alignment
- do not make the crowd below the same scale as rooftop figures
- do not fill the drop with random texture when height is the point

STYLE_VARIATION:
- realistic: more roof fixtures, drains, weathering, and safety details
- anime simplified: clean roof slab, bold parapet, a few vents, strong edge shadow
- gothic urban: darker ledge, heavier vertical void, rain-slick concrete
- neon city: colored billboard bounce, reflective roof puddles, glowing distant street
- redjuice-inspired: dark roof planes cut by surgical highlights and tiny city glyph accents

SCENE_USAGE:
- use for rooftop chases, high-city duels, falling-risk compositions, hero/villain confrontations, and skyline silhouettes
- pair with `building_facade`, `window_grid`, `transit_signage_glyph_panel`, `glass_reflection_panel`, and `city_crowd_below`

- Generalized rooftop drawing card focused on support plane, drop scale, and foot-contact believability

CONFIDENCE: high
