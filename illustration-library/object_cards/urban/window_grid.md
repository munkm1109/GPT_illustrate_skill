NAME: window grid
ALIASES: building windows; window band; repeated windows; facade windows; 창문 그리드; 창문 배열; 윈도우 밴드
CATEGORY: urban / architecture / repeated opening

FUNCTION:
- provides repeated scale and perspective rhythm on building facades, train cars, offices, apartments, and city corridors
- breaks large wall planes into believable human-scale modules
- helps the viewer read depth, floor height, and building orientation

CORE_SILHOUETTE:
- rows or columns of rectangular openings or lit panes aligned to one facade or vehicle plane
- reads as organized repetition, not random bright squares

PRIMARY_FORMS:
- window opening or glass pane
- mullion / frame divisions
- sill or ledge
- lintel / top frame
- optional curtains, blinds, interior dark mass, or reflected exterior shapes

DIMENSIONAL_ENVELOPE:
- individual windows vary widely but should usually read smaller than a door and larger than a head in near/mid distance
- rows stack by story height; bands follow the plane of the wall, tram, train, or tower
- in far background, multiple windows can compress into organized dotted or striped rhythm

THICKNESS_NOTES:
- window frames and mullions need a slight edge thickness in close or 3/4 views
- recessed windows need dark side planes or sill shadows
- glass panes can be flat but should still sit inside a frame or opening

PROPORTION_RULES:
- repeated windows in one row should share height and perspective
- spacing compresses with distance along the vanishing direction
- mullions are thinner than main frames, and frames are thinner than wall/floor bands
- random size variation should be reserved for damaged, old, or mixed-use facades

HUMAN_SCALE_READ:
- near windows can imply room, floor, and character scale; avoid making a standing adult taller than multiple story bands unless intentionally giant
- a human head usually reads far smaller than a full window opening in near building context
- window rows help keep vehicles from shrinking into toys

SUPPORT_LOGIC:
- windows sit inside facade, tram, train, or vehicle body planes
- frames attach to wall thickness; sills project outward and catch light
- lit windows imply interior depth, not free-floating light stickers

MATERIALS:
- glass, metal frame, wood frame, concrete/brick edge, plastic/acrylic panels
- wet glass catches vertical streaks and broken highlights; dark glass reflects surrounding lights

VISUAL_CUES:
- repeated rectangles
- aligned top/bottom edges
- mullion crossbars
- sill shadows
- lit/dark alternation
- reflected highlights following the same plane

ANGLE_NOTES:
- front: grid alignment and row spacing matter most
- side: frame thickness and recess shadows matter most
- 3/4: window rows must converge with the facade plane
- extreme distance: simplify to small grouped marks, but preserve row logic

DO_NOT_BREAK:
- do not scatter window marks without a shared plane
- do not let every pane become equally bright and focal
- do not place readable text inside tiny windows unless it is intentionally a sign, not a window
- do not use windows as random decoration when they should establish scale and perspective

STYLE_VARIATION:
- realistic: more mullions, curtains, interior darks, grime, and reflection changes
- anime simplified: fewer rows, larger dark/light blocks, cleaner frame rhythm
- gothic: taller narrow windows, deeper dark recesses, stronger verticals
- cyberpunk / neon: mixed lit windows, colored reflections, glass bands, and hard edge glows
- redjuice-inspired: sharp small highlights and dark window grids that support the focal face without equalizing contrast

SCENE_USAGE:
- pair with `building_facade` to lock scale and perspective
- use on tram/train cars to prevent vehicles from becoming small props
- use as depth rhythm in rail canyons, streets, alleys, and high-rise backgrounds
- suppress window contrast near the focal face unless the window is the intended frame

SOURCE_NOTES:
- Generalized from common architectural drawing and urban illustration practice: repeated openings as perspective and scale rulers
