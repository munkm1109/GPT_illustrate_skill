NAME: glass reflection panel
ALIASES: glass panel; reflective window; wet glass plane; shop glass; curtain wall glass; 유리 패널; 반사 유리; 쇼윈도
CATEGORY: urban / reflective surface

FUNCTION:
- creates controlled highlights, reflection fragments, and depth cues on buildings, stations, vehicles, storefronts, and interior partitions
- supports mood through glare, rain streaks, and partial reflections without becoming shapeless glow
- helps separate hard-surface planes from soft atmosphere

CORE_SILHOUETTE:
- flat rectangular or trapezoid plane with a frame, edge, or seam and reflection streaks aligned to its surface
- reads as a pane attached to architecture, not as floating light fog

PRIMARY_FORMS:
- glass face
- frame / mullion / gasket
- edge thickness
- reflected highlight shape
- dark interior or exterior reflected mass
- rain streaks, dirt, cracks, or condensation when relevant

DIMENSIONAL_ENVELOPE:
- can range from hand-sized small panes to full facade curtain-wall modules
- depth is shallow, but the frame and recess give real thickness
- in scenes, large panels often behave as vertical or slanted planes that mirror nearby lights and silhouettes

THICKNESS_NOTES:
- glass itself is thin, but frame, gasket, wall recess, and edge shadows provide visible structure
- cracked or broken glass needs shard thickness and fracture origin logic
- rain streaks should follow gravity on the glass plane unless wind or motion is dominant

PROPORTION_RULES:
- panel edges must align to the host wall, door, tram, train, or station plane
- reflection streaks follow the panel direction and lighting source, not random brush directions
- bright glare should occupy small controlled regions unless the panel is the focal object

HUMAN_SCALE_READ:
- storefront glass can exceed human height; smaller building windows relate to room modules
- reflections can show partial figure scale, but should not replace the actual body silhouette
- large glass panels make nearby people, signs, and vehicles feel grounded when edges and seams are clear

SUPPORT_LOGIC:
- glass is held by frames, mullions, brackets, gasket strips, door rails, or facade systems
- broken panels still leave shards attached to frames or falling from a believable break path
- reflective highlights must correspond to a light source or nearby bright object

MATERIALS:
- glass, acrylic, metal frames, rubber gasket, wet rain film, grime, cracks

VISUAL_CUES:
- hard straight edges
- frame or mullion
- partial reflection
- edge glints
- rain streaks
- cloudy dark interior mass
- cracks or shard lines only when story-relevant

ANGLE_NOTES:
- front: glare shape and transparency/darkness balance matter most
- side: thin edge and frame thickness matter most
- 3/4: reflection direction, trapezoid perspective, and frame depth matter most
- close-up: cracks, smears, and edge thickness can become focal texture

DO_NOT_BREAK:
- do not use generic glow with no panel edge
- do not let reflection marks ignore the panel perspective
- do not make glass equally bright everywhere unless it is deliberately overexposed
- do not use random shard texture where an intact panel is needed

STYLE_VARIATION:
- realistic: subtle transparency, layered reflection, dirt, and edge refraction
- anime simplified: one dark pane, a few clean highlight strips, and a clear frame
- gothic: cold black glass, white edge glints, cracked or cathedral-like divisions
- neon sci-fi: cyan/magenta reflected bands, HUD-like glows, and hard luminous seams
- redjuice-inspired: sharp glass facets and selective white/cyan flares inside dark architectural planes

SCENE_USAGE:
- use for shopfronts, station walls, high-rise curtain walls, vehicle windows, lab partitions, and rain-dark city surfaces
- useful for adding density while preserving plane logic
- keep glass glare subordinate to face/eyes unless it is intentionally the frame around the focal subject

SOURCE_NOTES:
- Generalized from common glass rendering and architectural-panel drawing logic: frame/edge first, reflection second, texture last

CONFIDENCE: high
