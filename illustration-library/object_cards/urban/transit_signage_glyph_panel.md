NAME: transit signage glyph panel
ALIASES: transit sign panel; station label panel; unreadable label block; route glyph panel; 교통 표지판; 역 표지판; 추상 교통 문자판; 글리프 간판
CATEGORY: urban / signage / text-safe panel

FUNCTION:
- provides urban transit identity, route logic, wayfinding mood, and small controlled accent blocks without requiring exact readable typography
- solves the common image-generation problem where tiny letters become broken by declaring signs as abstract glyph blocks unless exact text will be added later
- acts as a scale and perspective marker mounted to buildings, posts, tram stops, platforms, or station structures

CORE_SILHOUETTE:
- rectangular, square, vertical, or strip-like sign panel with border frame, bracket, and simplified glyph/label marks
- reads as mounted signage, not as floating random text fragments

PRIMARY_FORMS:
- sign face
- outer frame / bezel
- bracket, pole, wall mount, or hanging rail
- internal glyph rows, blocks, arrows, route numbers, or label rectangles
- optional glow face, grime, scratches, rain streaks, or reflection halo

DIMENSIONAL_ENVELOPE:
- small labels can be hand-to-head sized; route panels can be torso-to-door sized; overhead station signs can span multiple people
- thickness stays shallow compared with face area, but frame and bracket need visible depth
- tiny background signs should be simple blocks, not attempted full text

THICKNESS_NOTES:
- sign face is thin but rigid; frame is thicker than printed/glyph layer
- brackets and poles must be strong enough for panel size
- illuminated panels need shallow box depth or visible edge thickness

PROPORTION_RULES:
- panel scale must fit its mounting surface and surrounding architecture
- glyph marks should align in rows/columns and stay subordinate to the sign face
- do not fill every panel with equal-density marks; one or two rows often read better than fake paragraphs
- exact typography should be reserved for post-editing or explicitly large focal text

HUMAN_SCALE_READ:
- nearby small signs are roughly head/torso scale; overhead signs can be person-width or larger
- a sign mounted beside a door, tram, or window should reinforce rather than contradict object scale
- tiny labels in the far background should become abstract glyph blocks

SUPPORT_LOGIC:
- signs attach to walls, poles, gantries, station roofs, vehicle faces, or hanging rails
- wall signs need brackets, screws, frame edges, or shadow contact
- hanging signs need rods, cables, or rails that connect to a ceiling/gantry

MATERIALS:
- acrylic, painted metal, glass, LED panel, printed vinyl, weathered enamel, plastic frame
- lit signs create controlled glow; unlit signs catch edge light and rain streaks

VISUAL_CUES:
- border frame
- row-like glyph blocks
- arrow or route-number-like blocks when needed
- bracket or mount
- shallow box thickness
- small glow or reflection halo

ANGLE_NOTES:
- front: face layout and simple glyph rhythm matter most
- side: panel thickness and bracket support matter most
- 3/4: mount logic and perspective alignment to building/station plane matter most
- far distance: collapse text into block marks and preserve panel silhouette

DO_NOT_BREAK:
- do not attempt small readable typography in generated art unless it is large and focal
- do not scatter text-like marks directly on background without a panel or surface
- do not let signs float without brackets, wall contact, poles, or hanging supports
- do not make every sign equally bright, saturated, or detailed
- do not use fake text as a substitute for real object structure

STYLE_VARIATION:
- realistic: more weathering, mounting hardware, route layout hierarchy, and subtle dirt
- anime simplified: clear panel silhouette with a few glyph blocks and controlled color
- cyberpunk: stronger cyan/magenta/green light, stacked panels, and wet reflections
- gothic urban: darker metal frames, worn labels, and stricter white/red accent discipline
- redjuice-inspired: tiny controlled warning/route labels as accent planes, never competing with the face

SCENE_USAGE:
- use when a scene needs signage mood but exact text is not required
- ideal for rail stations, tram corridors, city alleys, platform scenes, futuristic interiors, and background worldbuilding
- pair with `building_facade`, `rail_track`, `tram_car`, `glass_reflection_panel`, and `cable_bundle`
- if exact words are required, plan a post-edit text overlay instead of relying on the generator

SOURCE_NOTES:

- Structure is generalized from transit panels, station signage, wayfinding boards, and urban sign mounting logic
