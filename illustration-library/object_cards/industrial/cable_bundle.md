NAME: cable bundle
ALIASES: wire bundle; conduit bundle; harness cluster; bundled cables; 케이블 번들; 전선 다발
CATEGORY: industrial / routing assembly

FUNCTION:
- routes power, data, or fluid-adjacent control lines through a space
- implies complexity, infrastructure, maintenance burden, and machine dependence

CORE_SILHOUETTE:
- several parallel lines gathered into a thick rope-like mass
- often curves in one dominant direction with smaller line breaks at the edges
- held by clips, straps, conduit sleeves, or support brackets

PRIMARY_FORMS:
- main cable mass
- outer tie points or clamps
- branch-off lines
- wall, ceiling, or machine anchor points
- optional conduit tray or sleeve

DIMENSIONAL_ENVELOPE:
- light bundle logic: about 20-40 mm overall bundle diameter
- medium routed bundle logic: about 40-80 mm diameter
- heavy trunk logic: about 80-180 mm diameter when multiple lines are grouped
- support clips observed across thin industrial edge conditions from about 0.5-8.0 mm panel thickness families

THICKNESS_NOTES:
- single visible cable lines should not all read the same thickness; use grouped outer thickness plus smaller branch lines
- edge clips and bundling bars can read around 10-45 mm in local hardware length depending on type
- sleeves or wraps should visibly increase bundle thickness over bare-wire groupings

PROPORTION_RULES:
- bundle thickness should taper only where branches split
- branch lines are always thinner than the main routed mass
- the longer the span, the more support or sag logic is required

HUMAN_SCALE_READ:
- thin bundles read like finger-to-wrist thickness
- medium bundles read like wrist-to-forearm thickness
- heavy trunk bundles can read like upper-arm thickness in industrial scenes

SUPPORT_LOGIC:
- the bundle must begin somewhere and terminate somewhere
- long unsupported spans should sag or be clipped, bracketed, or sleeved
- thicker bundles need thicker anchor points or trays
- branch lines should emerge from believable split locations, not random mid-air points
- edge routing should hug panels, frames, or machine boundaries when possible

MATERIALS:
- rubber or polymer insulation
- braided sleeve or conduit wrap
- plastic clips
- steel tray, bracket, or anchor rail

VISUAL_CUES:
- repeated tie straps
- connector heads
- layered thickness
- mild sag from gravity
- color coding or warning bands
- occasional branch splits
- edge clips or mount points at direction changes

ANGLE_NOTES:
- front: reads as layered line density and width variation
- side: depth stacking and sag are important
- 3/4: best for showing directional flow, attachment logic, and overlap rhythm

DO_NOT_BREAK:
- cable thickness must stay consistent enough to feel like a real routed system
- cables should not float with no origin or destination
- heavy bundles need visible support
- branching logic must remain readable
- clips and anchors should appear where routing direction changes or where weight needs support

STYLE_VARIATION:
- realistic: more connector variation, cable sleeves, support hardware, slight irregularity
- anime simplified: compress many small lines into grouped ribbons while keeping routing logic
- gothic: use cable bundles more like dark vine masses or iron-wrapped cords
- neon sci-fi: glowing strips, coded bands, luminous data conduits, cleaner directional arcs

SCENE_USAGE:
- ideal for gaze flow and depth layering around walls, ceilings, or lab equipment
- can frame the character without becoming the first read if kept lower contrast
- useful for making empty futuristic spaces feel inhabited and functional
- works as a connective visual motif between major machines

SOURCE_NOTES:
- Observed from HellermannTyton edge-clip and cable-routing materials: bundled routing along edges, clip-based mounting, no-drill fastening, and support logic for wires/conduits
- Additional structure is generalized from common industrial harness layouts rather than one proprietary bundle type
- Electrical specification is intentionally omitted; this card focuses on silhouette, routing, and support logic

CONFIDENCE: high
