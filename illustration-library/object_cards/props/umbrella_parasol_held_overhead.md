NAME: umbrella / parasol held overhead
ALIASES: umbrella; parasol; rain umbrella; black umbrella; 우산; 양산; 파라솔; held umbrella; overhead umbrella grip
CATEGORY: prop / handheld / canopy

FUNCTION:
- shields the figure from rain or moonlight while forming a circular/arched silhouette above the head
- can act as a halo, black-mass canopy, or framing device around the face and eye
- gives the visible hand a clear grip task and creates spokes/ribs that can point attention back to the face

CORE_SILHOUETTE:
- domed or shallow-cone canopy above or behind the head, with a straight central shaft descending to the hand
- outer rim reads as a curved arc; ribs radiate from the top cap toward rim points
- handle may end in a hook, knob, or straight grip depending on style

PRIMARY_FORMS:
- canopy dome/cone surface
- top cap/ferrule point
- radial rib lines under canopy
- central shaft cylinder
- handle grip or hook
- hand contact ring around the shaft

DIMENSIONAL_ENVELOPE:
- common open canopy width is roughly shoulder-width to wider-than-shoulders; for stylized framing it may span 1.5-2.5 head widths
- shaft runs from canopy center through or beside the hand; visible length depends on crop
- canopy depth should read as an ellipse in perspective, not a flat semicircle

THICKNESS_NOTES:
- ribs are thin but visible at the underside, thicker at the central hub and rim joints
- shaft is a slim cylinder; avoid making it as thick as a weapon unless intentionally hybridized
- canopy fabric has thin edge thickness, with slight scallop or broken rim if torn

PROPORTION_RULES:
- hub sits near the center of the canopy ellipse/cone; ribs radiate evenly but perspective compresses far-side spacing
- handle aligns with shaft axis; hand wraps around it with thumb opposing fingers
- canopy rim should clear the head unless intentionally tilted behind it

HUMAN_SCALE_READ:
- an open umbrella held above a standing adult reads larger than the head and usually wider than the shoulders
- hand grip sits near chest, shoulder, or head level depending on pose; wrist should not float away from the shaft

SUPPORT_LOGIC:
- canopy weight is carried through the shaft into the gripping hand, then through wrist/forearm/shoulder
- tilted umbrella requires wrist angle and shoulder lift that match the shaft angle
- if wind/rain is implied, canopy and hair should lean in compatible directions

MATERIALS:
- fabric canopy: matte or semi-matte, can show wet sheen and scratch texture
- ribs/shaft: metal or lacquered black, hard narrow highlights
- handle: black plastic, wood, or metal hook

VISUAL_CUES:
- radial ribs
- curved rim/ellipse
- central cap
- straight shaft
- hand wrapping around handle/shaft
- rain beads, tears, or ragged rim when dramatic

ANGLE_NOTES:
- front view: canopy reads as arch/ellipse above head with symmetric or slightly tilted ribs
- 3/4 view: one side of rim appears closer/larger; far-side ribs compress
- low angle: underside ribs and hub are visible, canopy becomes a strong halo/frame
- side view: canopy narrows and shaft angle becomes dominant

DO_NOT_BREAK:
- do not draw the canopy as a flat paper circle with no ellipse or rib logic
- do not let ribs ignore the hub point
- do not let the hand hold empty air away from the shaft
- do not scatter ribs so they compete with the face; arrange them as gaze-flow lines

STYLE_VARIATION:
- anime simplified: reduce ribs to 5-8 clear lines and keep the rim graphic
- gothic/dark action: black canopy can merge with hair/coat mass while cyan rim light separates the face
- grunge: scratch fabric, ragged rim, rain speckles, chipped metal shaft
- symbolic: canopy can become moon/halo-like or skull/chain-framed if structure remains readable

SCENE_USAGE:
- good as overhead frame, halo substitute, rain shelter, and black-mass device
- pairs well with chains, moon backlight, ruined walls, checker floors, and electric eye accents
- visible hand/finger grip should be researched/applied when it is near the face
