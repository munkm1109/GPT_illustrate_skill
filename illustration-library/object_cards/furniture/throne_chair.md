NAME: throne chair
ALIASES: throne; regal chair; seat of state; 왕좌; 옥좌
CATEGORY: furniture / ceremonial seating

FUNCTION:
- establishes authority, hierarchy, and ritual centrality
- anchors the scene as the dominant support object for a ruler-like seated figure

CORE_SILHOUETTE:
- high back chair with broad presence, armrest structure, and a raised seating platform or dais relationship
- reads as taller, stiffer, and more formal than a normal lounge chair

PRIMARY_FORMS:
- tall backrest
- seat platform
- armrests
- leg or plinth support
- optional crest, finial, or carved top accent

DIMENSIONAL_ENVELOPE:
- chair height should exceed seated shoulder line
- seat width should feel generous but not sofa-wide
- armrests should clearly frame the body without enclosing it completely

THICKNESS_NOTES:
- back and arm structures should feel substantial and load-bearing
- seat edge needs enough depth to support a lifted-knee pose
- dais or stair-top platform should feel heavier than the chair alone

PROPORTION_RULES:
- vertical emphasis is stronger than in ordinary chairs
- backrest should clearly dominate above the seated head or shoulder line depending on design
- armrests should align with a seated elbow or lower-rib zone, not float randomly

HUMAN_SCALE_READ:
- unmistakably a seat of authority sized for one person
- should frame the character and make nearby prostrated people feel lower in rank and altitude

SUPPORT_LOGIC:
- the throne must sit stably on a landing or dais
- lifted-knee poses require enough seat depth and/or side support to avoid a floating seated read
- if placed atop stairs, the chair base and the stair landing must align believably

MATERIALS:
- carved wood, lacquered wood, metal trim, textile cushion, or stone/wood hybrid logic

VISUAL_CUES:
- high back
- armrest framing
- ceremonial symmetry or near-symmetry
- carved trim or crest detail
- raised position relative to the room

ANGLE_NOTES:
- front: authority and symmetry read best
- 45-degree view: strongest for showing seat depth and armrest support with the character pose
- side: requires extra care so the backrest still feels monumental

DO_NOT_BREAK:
- do not let it read like a dining chair or casual armchair
- seat support and back height must feel authoritative
- throne silhouette must remain readable behind the character and costume volume

STYLE_VARIATION:
- realistic: carved details, cushion compression, heavier joinery
- anime simplified: bold backrest, armrest, and dais silhouette with selective ornament
- gothic: darker wood, gold accents, sharp crest language, velvet or lacquer richness
- sacred-tech fantasy: preserve the throne hierarchy while introducing symbolic glow or metallic motifs

SCENE_USAGE:
- central anchor for authority scenes
- ideal for arranging a lifted-knee seated pose with strong hierarchy
- can remain darker than the face while still giving the composition ritual structure

- Structural notes are generalized from throne/seated-of-state references: high back, armrests, and dais-based hierarchy
- Optimized for illustration blocking rather than furniture conservation detail
