# THEORY_01_INTENT

Summary: Define the scene before drawing it. The goal of this theory is to lock a scene into one concrete line so composition, value, and expression do not drift.

Summary: Step 1 must answer who the subject is, what state they are in, what they are doing, what 1-2 emotions dominate, and what the viewer should feel first.

## Purpose

Create a one-line scene intent before any composition or rendering decisions.
This line is the control sentence for later stages.

## Core concepts

### Scene intent

Scene intent is a sentence that summarizes:

- who is in the scene
- what state they are in
- what they are doing
- what emotional moment is being shown

Example shape:

- "In a neon laboratory, a girl looks down with a cold smile during a tense moment of control."

### Emotion axis

Use one or two major emotions only.
Examples:

- danger + allure
- sorrow + anger
- calm + anxiety

### Role and context

Emotion alone is not enough.
Define why the character feels that way by clarifying role and situation.

Examples:

- role: experimenter, victim, observer, commander, bystander
- relationship: dominant, exposed, hiding, waiting, discovered

## Required question set

Before writing the intent line, answer:

1. When is this scene?
2. Where is this scene?
3. What is the character doing right now?
4. What 1-2 emotions dominate?
5. What should the viewer feel first?

## Intent sentence patterns

Use one of these patterns.

### Pattern A

`[environment] + [character/role] + [action] + [emotion] moment`

### Pattern B

`[time/lighting] + [environment] + [role] + [expression/attitude] revealing [emotion]`

### Pattern C

`show [character] in [situation] so the viewer feels [audience feeling]`

## Good intent line rules

- Prefer one sentence, maximum two.
- Include the viewer feeling explicitly.
- Avoid vague adjectives such as "pretty", "cool", or "nice".
- Prefer concrete nouns, verbs, and atmosphere words.

Helpful word types:

- nouns: lab bench, neon tube, cage, blade, blood, cathedral, window frame
- verbs: look down, wait, hide, endure, drop, threaten
- atmosphere words: damp, cold, quiet, sticky, flashing

## Downstream links

Step 1 output must inform:

- Step 2 composition
- Step 3 lighting and value
- Step 4 expression and pose restraint

Examples:

- dominance / threat -> stronger angle control, more pressure in composition
- isolation / anxiety -> off-center placement and more empty space
- danger / tension -> stronger contrast and colder ambient light with selective warm accents
- restrained emotion -> smaller body motion and subtler face movement

## Execution summary for Step 1

1. Answer the 5 questions.
2. Write one intent line with Pattern A, B, or C.
3. Tag:
   - environment
   - time_or_lighting
   - role
   - action
   - emotion_axis
   - audience_feeling
4. Pass the tagged data forward to Steps 2, 3, and 4.

## Gate

Do not continue to Step 2 unless all are true:

- the subject is clearly identifiable
- where / when / what is visible in the sentence
- emotion is narrowed to 1-2 items
- audience feeling is explicit
- the sentence is imageable through concrete nouns or verbs

If any item fails, rewrite the intent line.
